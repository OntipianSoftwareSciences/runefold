What is a spike?

A neuron is like a tiny bucket with a leak. Charge drips in from other neurons, and charge slowly leaks out. If enough charge arrives at once, the bucket tips over — the neuron fires one short spike to the neurons it is connected to, then it needs a short rest. Most of the time most neurons are quiet. That sparseness is why a real brain runs on about 20 watts, while a big AI on a GPU uses hundreds.

What is Hebbian learning?

“Neurons that fire together, wire together.” If neuron A spikes just before neuron B, the connection A → B gets a little stronger. Do it again and again and the connection becomes strong enough that A alone can wake B up. That is how this brain learns pink + puppy → happy: we show it the inputs and the right answer together, 12 times, and the links in between grow.

What does sleep do?

Sleep tidies the brain. Links that were used a lot get a little stronger. Links that were never used fade, and very weak unused ones are pruned away. But a link you explicitly taught — like pink + puppy → happy — is protected: sleep never deletes something you taught on purpose. Same rule as Runefold's /consolidate.

Honest note: this is a toy — 12 neurons. It is not a real brain, not AGI, and it does not train the big language model. It is a small, visible way to learn the ideas a real brain-like AI would need. Scarlet's long-term direction is for Runefold's brain to one day help people talk again and walk again — assistive neurotech. That is a very long, clinical path, needing real brain-computer-interface research and clinicians. This v0.1 is step one: a brain you can watch learn one tiny thing.
